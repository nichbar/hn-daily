# Today's Highlights

Over the past four years, a quiet yet punishing contraction has gripped American creative professions: across film, television, journalism, and the performing arts, more than 200,000 jobs have vanished, with nearly 50,000 lost in the last twelve months alone. In modern American history, an employment slump of this severity has occurred only during the broad recessions of 2001 and 2008. Yet the broader economy today is not in recession; instead, the downturn has arrived alongside the rapid rise of generative artificial intelligence capable of producing prose, illustrations, and music at negligible marginal cost. Today's most discussed story, an in-depth investigation by macroeconomic research outlet [Apricitas Economics](https://www.apricitas.io/p/ai-and-the-fall-of-the-creative-class), cuts through the apocalyptic headlines to examine whether we are truly witnessing the fall of the creative class. The analysis reveals that the primary drivers are rooted in structural business cycles—the hangover from streaming overexpansion, aggressive studio consolidation, and production offshoring—with AI functioning less as an outright replacement for human talent and more as a convenient corporate catalyst for headcount reduction.

This tension between technological change, institutional incentives, and human labor echoes across today’s digest. In regulatory and public policy debates, Brazilian civil society groups accused [Meta of violating judicial orders by skewing platform algorithms toward a far-right presidential campaign](https://www.panamericandispatch.org/p/meta-ignores-brazilian-court-order), demonstrating the fragility of democratic oversight over opaque recommendation feeds. In Oregon, local police faced national outrage after demanding [nearly $2.5 million in public records fees to release misconduct files regarding a single officer](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/01/oregon-police-misconduct-public-records-cost-fee/), illustrating how bureaucratic cost estimates can be weaponized against investigative journalism. In medicine and science, a practicing dermatologist demonstrated that [a ten-year-old convolutional neural network substantially outperforms state-of-the-art frontier models at diagnosing melanoma](https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer), offering a sobering lesson in the limitations of general-purpose AI for safety-critical healthcare. On the engineering front, semiconductor startup Cerebras detailed how its [wafer-scale engines run multi-trillion parameter language models](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/how-we-deploy-trillion-parameter-frontier-models-on-cerebras) without the communication bottlenecks of traditional GPU clusters; an ambitious open-source project delivered a complete [modern cross-platform recreation of the Heroes of Might and Magic II engine](https://github.com/ihhub/fheroes2); and an independent monitoring station shared a continuous feed [tracking American nuclear command broadcasts on shortwave radio](https://missileer.watch/). Finally, a Fields Medal-winning mathematician at UC Berkeley made waves by [abandoning graded homework after automated tools made perfect scores trivial](https://www.dailycal.org/news/campus/academics/ai-use-leads-uc-berkeley-math-professor-to-see-no-point-in-grading-homework/article_bdf1eae2-c08b-491f-b9f1-81a7cea177de.html), forcing educators to confront what university assessment actually measures.

## Business and Platforms

### America has lost 200k of jobs in media, film and the arts. How much is AI?

In a detailed macroeconomic report titled [AI and the Fall? of the Creative Class](https://www.apricitas.io/p/ai-and-the-fall-of-the-creative-class), analyst Joseph Politano investigates the severe labor downturn across American cultural industries. Over the last four years, employment across film, sound recording, broadcasting, and publishing dropped by more than 200,000 positions, matching the job losses of the 2001 dot-com bust and the 2008 financial crisis. While the public often attributes this collapse entirely to generative AI, the report demonstrates that deeper economic currents are responsible. The post-pandemic hangover from overextended streaming budgets, aggressive Hollywood corporate mergers, and the widespread migration of physical production to subsidized overseas hubs have drastically compressed domestic opportunities. Furthermore, strict copyright doctrine denying protection to purely machine-generated work makes major studios cautious about fully substituting human staff, meaning AI currently operates primarily as management leverage to squeeze operational costs rather than an autonomous replacement for human creativity.

Note: The Hacker News discussion broadly welcomed the nuanced economic breakdown, with contributors pushing back against the narrative that technology is the sole culprit. Readers with entertainment backgrounds noted that corporate consolidation in Hollywood mirrors patterns in manufacturing, where chasing short-term quarterly savings by moving production out of traditional hubs permanently erodes local talent ecosystems and institutional knowledge. Commenters also pointed out that corporate efficiency gains rarely translate into reduced working hours or higher compensation for creative staff, with the financial windfalls flowing almost exclusively to platform executives and equity holders. ## Policy and Governance

### Meta Ignores Brazilian Court Order to End Bolsonaro Favoritism

Civil society watchdogs Sleeping Giants Brasil and CTRL+Z filed an urgent petition before Brazil’s Superior Electoral Court, alleging that [Meta continues to favor far-right political candidates across Instagram and WhatsApp](https://www.panamericandispatch.org/p/meta-ignores-brazilian-court-order) in violation of a standing judicial mandate. The filing contends that neutral user searches systematically surface favorable algorithmic recommendations for Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, while associating left-wing President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva with derogatory hashtags and stickers. The electoral court had previously instructed the tech giant to guarantee strictly equal algorithmic visibility across presidential campaigns. The petition demands immediate corrective measures within 24 hours, alongside an order compelling Meta to disclose the internal ranking rules and automated completion mechanisms governing political keywords.

Note: The thread on Hacker News captured sharp skepticism toward both Big Tech opacity and the petitioners’ methodology. Some commenters dismissed portions of the evidence—such as prompting Meta AI to pick a random number between 1 and 25, which returned party ballot identifiers—as statistical noise that distracts from genuine algorithmic accountability. However, experienced software engineers countered that regional recommendation weights in closed social networks are entirely shielded from outside verification, making covert throttling or algorithmic bias trivially easy to execute. The consensus among technical readers emphasized that relying on proprietary, multi-billion-user platforms as primary civic forums leaves democratic institutions alarmingly ill-equipped to enforce public interest standards. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50229587)

### Oregon Police Are Charging a Local News Outlet $2.5M for Records About One Cop

Public record transparency met an astonishing bureaucratic barrier in Oregon after local news outlet Double Sided Media filed a public records request regarding Eugene police officer Justin Peckels. The officer has accumulated repeated citizen complaints, including a documented traffic stop in which he punched a motorist in the face and fractured the man’s nose. In response to the newsroom’s request for disciplinary and investigative files spanning 2018 through 2026, the Eugene Police Department issued an estimate claiming that reviewing and redacting the records would require at least 44,816 staff hours, demanding [an exorbitant $2,482,806 fee to process the request](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/01/oregon-police-misconduct-public-records-cost-fee/). The Freedom of the Press Foundation condemned the decision, arguing that calculating astronomical labor fees functions as a de facto economic blackout against independent journalists pursuing legitimate public-interest reporting.

Note: Hacker News readers responded with deep frustration, sharing widespread personal experiences with government secrecy. Several commenters described a parallel administrative tactic: state agencies responding monthly with minimal batches of heavily redacted pages to stretch requests over years until community attention fades. Technical contributors argued that when public agencies are legally permitted to bill arbitrary hourly rates without independent cost review, freedom-of-information laws become functionally useless for small newsrooms and ordinary citizens. The thread voiced strong support for legislative caps on record retrieval fees and meaningful sanctions against municipal departments that fabricate compliance estimates to evade oversight. ## Science and Research

### A 10-year-old CNN outperforms frontier LLMs for skin cancer diagnosis

In an empirical clinical evaluation, a practicing dermatologist demonstrated that a decade-old specialized computer vision model decisively outperformed the most capable modern multi-modal language models at identifying life-threatening melanoma. Testing frontier models GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 against clinical photographs and microscopic dermoscopy images of an ear melanoma, the physician found that both flagship models incorrectly diagnosed the lesion as a benign seborrheic keratosis, advising that the patient could be safely discharged. By contrast, a fine-tuned implementation of Inception v3—a convolutional neural network (CNN), an architecture designed specifically to detect local visual patterns like edge irregularity and pigment networks—[correctly identified the invasive melanoma](https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer). The author noted that while general-purpose models excel at fluent text interaction, they lack the inductive spatial bias and domain calibration required for high-stakes pathological image analysis.

Note: The study sparked an engaging architectural debate on Hacker News regarding generalist models versus specialized classifiers. Multiple machine learning practitioners argued that rushing to deploy large multimodal models as universal diagnostic tools represents a classic case of misapplied technology, pointing out that dedicated convolutional classifiers remain far more reliable, computationally efficient, and verifiable for bounded visual tasks. Commenters strongly warned against consumer-facing AI medical advice, cautioning that when diagnostic chatbots emit confidently false reassurance in clinical settings, the real-world consequence is delayed treatment and preventable patient harm. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50233779)

## Tech and Products

### Cerebras Deploys Trillion-Parameter AI Models

Semiconductor firm Cerebras published an architectural overview demonstrating how it deploys multi-trillion parameter language models on its specialized wafer-scale hardware. Unlike conventional chipmaking, where silicon wafers are diced into hundreds of individual graphic processing units (GPUs) linked across complex clusters, Cerebras utilizes an entire uncut silicon wafer (the WSE-3T) as a monolithic computing engine containing hundreds of thousands of cores and massive on-chip SRAM bandwidth. In an engineering writeup titled [How Cerebras Deploys Trillion-Parameter AI Models](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/how-we-deploy-trillion-parameter-frontier-models-on-cerebras), the company detailed how it partitions open models like the 3-trillion parameter Kimi K3 across a handful of wafer systems. By streaming weights across dedicated wafer fabrics and eliminating the cross-server networking latency that bottlenecks traditional GPU clusters, the system achieves generation throughput between 1,000 and 2,000 tokens per second at scale.

Note: Technical commenters on Hacker News lauded the sheer physical audacity of wafer-scale computing while raising pragmatic concerns regarding commercial access and software maturity. Several enterprise practitioners noted that Cerebras’s high-throughput hardware is primarily tailored to bespoke deployments for well-capitalized cloud providers, remaining largely out of reach for individual researchers and startups. Hardware analysts in the thread observed that Nvidia’s enduring competitive advantage lies in its comprehensive CUDA ecosystem and flexible deployment primitives; until alternative hardware vendors provide open, reliable developer tooling, specialized architectures will struggle to unseat the prevailing GPU standard. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50227356)

### fheroes2: A recreation of Heroes of Might and Magic II game engine

A team of dedicated open-source developers has successfully reverse-engineered and modernized one of the defining PC strategy games of the 1990s. The [fheroes2](https://github.com/ihhub/fheroes2) project provides a clean-room C++ engine recreation of Heroes of Might and Magic II, allowing the turn-based classic to run natively across modern operating systems including Linux, macOS, Windows, Android, and handheld gaming consoles. While remaining meticulously faithful to original combat formulas, resource mechanics, and tactical logic, the new engine introduces modern quality-of-life enhancements: native support for ultra-widescreen 4K displays, a smarter and more competitive artificial intelligence opponent, and tailored touch controls. To respect intellectual property rights, the project provides only the open-source executable code, requiring players to supply original data files from legitimate retail copies.

Note: The release sparked an outpouring of nostalgia and technical admiration across the Hacker News community. Longtime turn-based strategy enthusiasts shared memories of hotseat multiplayer sessions on vintage hardware, praising the development team for rescuing gaming heritage from obsolete MS-DOS runtimes without sacrificing the artful balance of the original design. Several engineers in the thread shared playful experiments involving autonomous AI agents attempting to play the game, observing that managing long-range economic trade-offs across dozens of turns remains a formidable challenge for contemporary reasoning models. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50230384)

### Listen and read USA nuclear forces Emergency Action Messages, heard on 11175 kHz

Decades after the end of the Cold War, the voice channels governing global strategic nuclear deterrence remain remarkably active. An independent online audio project called [Missileer Watch](https://missileer.watch/) tracks and transcribes the High Frequency Global Communications System (HF-GCS) operated by the United States Air Force on its primary international frequency of 11175 kHz. Using a distributed network of software-defined radio (SDR) receivers—hardware that converts radio frequencies into digital data for computer processing—the platform automatically captures and logs Emergency Action Messages (EAMs). These preformatted broadcasts follow a rigid structure dating back to the mid-twentieth century: a station call sign, a three-times repeated warning, a six-character phonetic preamble, and a string of encrypted ciphertext. Dispatched from Strategic Command headquarters, these transmissions serve as the survivable communications backbone to nuclear submarines, strategic bombers, and intercontinental ballistic missile silos.

Note: The discussion on Hacker News unraveled the technical resilience and human atmosphere surrounding shortwave military communications. Radio hobbyists clarified that the frequency is far from dormant during peacetime, carrying routine connectivity checks and equipment tests multiple times a day. Commenters noted that in an era where undersea fiber cables and orbital satellites face growing electronic and physical threats, atmospheric ionospheric reflection provides an irreplaceable fallback that requires no fragile infrastructure. Listening to the cool, mechanical cadence of synthetic voices cutting through shortwave static served as a stark reminder of the enduring machinery underwriting modern global security. ## Society and Culture

### AI use leads UC Berkeley math professor to see 'no point' in grading homework

The accelerating proficiency of generative artificial intelligence in formal reasoning is upending foundational assumptions in university education. Richard Borcherds, a distinguished mathematics professor at UC Berkeley and recipient of the Fields Medal, announced in a campus interview that [he will no longer grade student homework](https://www.dailycal.org/news/campus/academics/ai-use-leads-uc-berkeley-math-professor-to-see-no-point-in-grading-homework/article_bdf1eae2-c08b-491f-b9f1-81a7cea177de.html). Borcherds explained that because students routinely utilize advanced AI tools to submit flawless problem sets, paying graduate teaching assistants to review homework has become futile. Instead, he now awards completion credit for submissions and weights course evaluation entirely on in-person, closed-book examinations. The mathematician expressed candid uncertainty regarding what advice to offer prospective mathematics students, noting that AI systems are advancing toward human-level theorem proving at a pace that mirrors historical industrial disruptions like the mechanization of hand-weaving.

Note: The announcement resonated powerfully across Hacker News, sparking a rigorous critique of transactional dynamics in modern higher education. Numerous contributors observed that homework assignments were originally intended as low-stakes learning sandboxes where intellectual friction leads to genuine conceptual mastery; in an environment obsessed with GPA optimization, grading transformed problem sets into a currency exchange that invites automated cheating. Top comments praised Borcherds for cutting through institutional denial, arguing that universities should replace automated homework grading with oral defenses, interactive blackboard problem-solving, and Socratic seminars that nurture irreplaceable human critical thinking.
