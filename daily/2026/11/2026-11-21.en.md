## Science and Research

### A claimed Riemann proof gets a formal-verification endorsement

Mathematician Levent Alpöge recently released a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis as a [Lean library of roughly 17 million lines](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/) (Lean is a tool that lets computers check mathematical proofs line by line), with methods said to extend to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. The Lean and Mathlib communities then organized verification and published a joint statement: the statement proved matches the standard formulation, the code compiles reproducibly, and the exported proof passed the official proof checker plus several independent verification tools. The Riemann Hypothesis, open since 1859, governs the deep pattern of prime numbers; if the proof holds, it is both a pure-mathematics milestone and evidence that AI-assisted assaults on top-tier problems are becoming a real path. The thread swings between witnessing-history excitement and scholarly caution: some readers feel humanity is entering an era of artifacts it no longer fully understands, while others caution that passing checks is not the same as mathematicians absorbing the proof's ideas. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50589618).

### A pancreatic cancer pill brings rare good news in decades

[The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/nov/20/pill-pancreatic-cancer-breakthrough-daraxonrasib) that an oral drug called daraxonrasib is being hailed as the biggest treatment breakthrough for pancreatic cancer in decades. It is a KRAS inhibitor, a drug class that latches onto mutations carried by most pancreatic cancers and shuts off the growth signal. In the trial, tumors stopped growing in nearly nine in ten patients, shrank or vanished in over six in ten, and became undetectable on CT scans in about one in twelve; one-year survival far exceeded the historical rate for chemotherapy alone. The researchers stress that longer follow-up is needed to confirm durable remission, and the same mutations appear in other cancers. The thread's mood is cautiously optimistic: several readers warned that trial cohorts skew toward late-stage patients with no alternatives, so "breakthrough" should be reserved for lasting remission, while others felt a signal this strong in such patients is genuinely worth celebrating. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50582069).

### A guitarist turns asteroid photos into stereo views

The European Space Agency's planetary-defense mission Hera sent back early images of the Didymos binary asteroid system, and team member Brian May, the Queen guitarist who is also an astrophysicist, combined them with a collaborator into [stereoscopic views](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera/sir-brian-may-adds-new-dimension-to-hera-asteroid). With 3D glasses or the cross-eyed technique, anyone can see the sharp equatorial ridge against the rubble-strewn surface. Hera aims to validate asteroid-deflection technology for planetary defense; the stereo pairs double as outreach and as an intuitive terrain check for scientists. Commenters were charmed: several noted May is a published astrophysicist, others swapped tips for free-viewing stereo images, and many agreed that turning deep-space reconnaissance into photos anyone can see in 3D is outreach at its best. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50583768).

## Tech and Products

### A monitor that ate three-quarters of the download speed

[A debugging memoir](https://blog.tymscar.com/posts/studiodisplayethernet/) tells a wonderfully counterintuitive story: home downloads ran at a quarter of the paid bandwidth, the ISP and router checked out, and the culprit turned out to be the monitor. The author had plugged a 2.5G Ethernet adapter into the display's dock for a one-cable setup, while a compatibility quirk in the monitor's built-in USB expansion chip pinned throughput at the slow lane; plugging straight into the computer restored full speed. The fun is how far the fault sits from intuition: who suspects the screen of throttling the network. The thread quickly became a victims' reunion, with readers confessing weeks lost to docks and dongles; the engineering takeaway, several argued, is that display makers economize on expansion features, so bypass every middleman first when debugging networks. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50587875).

### An open recipe for squeezing big models onto laptops

The open-source project [LookAtMyQuant](https://github.com/ShinMegamiBoson/lookatmyquant) demonstrates a quantization-compiler approach, quantization being the art of shrinking a model to fit smaller memory. The method measures how much each stored piece hurts output quality when compressed, then budgets codes across the whole model to preserve capability at a smaller size. In one example, a multi-billion-parameter model went from over 50 GB to 7.2 GB while matching the original on math problems and beating same-size mainstream formats; an even smaller build reaches 5.4 GB and runs briskly on a laptop. The digest's reading: models are turning from cloud services into files a person can own, and local execution means lower cost and better privacy. Commenters focused on measured reality: some complained of slower-than-expected speeds on their own GPUs, others argued that is a kernel-optimization issue rather than a flaw in the method, with reproducible open recipes mattering more than any single model. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50591853).

### A serious crypto-library flaw: patch first, argue later

[GnuPG's security advisory](https://lists.gnupg.org/pipermail/gnupg-announce/2026q4/000517.html) reports a critical bug in all prior versions of the GPGME library, a shared component used by many encryption programs: a crafted overlong user ID in a key can trigger a double-free memory error, where the same block of memory is wrongly released twice, corrupting the program's state, potentially exfiltrating application data, with remote code execution not ruled out. Version 2.3.1 fixes it and the project urges immediate upgrades. Because GPGME feeds many mail clients and signing tools, one upstream fix ripples across the ecosystem. The thread's main split was familiar: some called labeling a maybe-RCE as RCE overstated and demanded a working exploit chain, while others countered that treating memory-corruption bugs as worst-case by default is the responsible posture, patch first, debate after. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50587208).

## Policy and Governance

### Brazil open-sources its tax calculator

[Brazil's federal tax authority announced](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2026/novembro/receita-federal-publica-primeiro-codigo-fonte-aberto-no-github-e-amplia-transparencia-da-reforma-tributaria) it has published the source code of its tax-reform calculator on GitHub under the MIT license, a permissive open-source license, the agency's first open-source release. With old and new tax regimes running in parallel during a sweeping reform, the calculation rules are fiendishly complex; publishing the core logic lets businesses, accountants, and ordinary citizens check how any tax bill was actually computed. Commenters were overwhelmingly supportive: several argued that government software citizens are forced to use should be open by default, or it amounts to a hidden tax, while pragmatic voices added that open-sourcing is only step one, and sustained maintenance plus real community collaboration will test the sincerity. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50532613).

### A backup vendor rewrites its privacy terms; longtime users vote with their feet

Cloud-backup provider [Backblaze updated its privacy policy and collection notice](https://www.backblaze.com/company/policy/privacy) around opt-outs for the sale and sharing of user information, drawing a backlash. Several readers described the opt-out flow as a multi-hop dark pattern, longtime customers voiced disappointment, and some reported migrating to rival backup services or self-hosted open-source tooling. The broader point stands on its own: cheap cloud storage often prices data rights into the fine print, and every privacy-terms update deserves a close read. The thread's keywords were disappointment and moving day: some posted self-rescue opt-out links, others compared alternatives, and a resigned camp admitted ordinary people must trade convenience against privacy, insisting the choice be genuinely usable rather than buried three menus deep. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50585593).

## Society and Culture

### When the AI look becomes a style you can install

[Slopstrap bills itself](https://slopstrap.mikelacher.com/) as more than a CSS framework: an accelerant for making any site look hastily generated by a language model, with dark mode, ambient orbs, and "AI engine operational" badges out of the box. Its popularity nails a real fatigue: countless AI-built landing pages share the same purple gradients and floating blobs, and the author lands a precise critique through parody. Once generative tools make polish cheap, taste itself becomes the scarce good. The thread split between jokes and earnestness: some said the framework satirizes exactly what its readers ship daily, while others argued templates are not the villain, and the real problem is treating "looks AI-made" as a shipping standard. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50576012).

### An engineer unemployed for two years, and hundreds of replies about the future

A US-based senior software engineer with fifteen years of experience wrote in [a help-seeking post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50583761) that two years without work had nearly exhausted his savings, applications went nowhere, and even a pizzeria manager job was on the table. The post became an open debate on engineers' worth in the AI age: one camp holds that writing code is automated and orchestrating AI workflows is the new skill, while the other counters that AI output is brittle and real engineering judgment is worth more than ever. Practical advice filled the middle: compress the resume gap, build side projects, stabilize cash flow first. The thread's most grounded voices said to land any job that feeds the family before theorizing about the long term; the discussion long ago outgrew one person's plea and became a field sample of structural change in tech employment. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50583761).

### The body drives first; persuasion comes later

[An essay on mind and body](https://notnottalmud.substack.com/p/getting-the-mind-to-steer-the-body) starts from a legendary quit-smoking book: countless readers claimed the book alone cured their addiction, not through willpower but by reframing the craving itself, what you want gone is the craving, not the cigarette. The author generalizes: many stalemates in life come from the body driving while reason shouts from the back seat, and no pep talk reaches it until you find the words the body obeys. Commenters turned the thread into a reading-list swap, from Gallwey's Inner Game books to Zen in the Art of Archery, converging on the view that the trick is a new angle of observation rather than more force; several added the survivor-bias caveat that epiphany stories still need personal testing.
