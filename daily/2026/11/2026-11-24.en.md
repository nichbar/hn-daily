# Today's Highlights

The most delightful surprise of the day lives inside a browser tab: [trynix.dev](https://fzakaria.com/2026/11/23/2035-came-early) runs hundreds of thousands of Linux programs with no install step, even a full Firefox nested inside itself, as if an old prophecy about software moving into the web arrived years early. Around it, the day's arguments all circle the same question of who technology really serves. An AI assistant refuses to help its user buy music from indie artists, New York outlaws subscription fees for car hardware buyers already own, on-chain sleuths expose a suspiciously well-timed bet on a prediction market, and a star AI listing slips below its IPO price. From the browser to the garage to the courtroom, this edition is about who holds the keys.

## Tech and Products

### A whole desktop inside a browser tab

Developer Farhan Zakaria built [trynix.dev](https://fzakaria.com/2026/11/23/2035-came-early) on top of the Nix software collection so that more than 300,000 Linux packages run straight from a web page. A new engine called unkernel compiles the Linux kernel into a format the browser can execute, delivering two to thirty-two times the speed of the old approach, with graphics, sound, and networking included. Clicking a link now opens solitaire, a terminal, or a complete Firefox, and the author went one level deeper by opening the site again inside that in-browser Firefox. For a general reader, the takeaway is simple: installing software may soon be optional.

Note: the thread split over JavaScript's legacy, with several readers confessing the language once drove them into management and that AI now spares them from ever looking at it again, while others pushed back that it is a fine tool blamed by people who never learned it. A second strand asked whether AI is draining the joy out of programming itself, with two kinds of nostalgia rhyming unexpectedly. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50624497).

### A pocket-sized local model starts taking real jobs

PrismML released [Ternary Bonsai 2.5](https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-5-27b), a model built for on-device use: roughly 5.9 GB in size, yet much closer to its full-precision base on coding and multi-step tool use. The company says the new version cured its predecessor's habit of overthinking in loops, finishing tasks faster and cheaper, and it runs smoothly even on ordinary consumer graphics cards. The pitch is an assistant that reads private files and handles chores without sending data to the cloud. For anyone uneasy about where personal data travels, every step forward for small models like this deserves a look.

Note: top comments treated the benchmarks with suspicion, with one reader noting results near early-year frontier models and wondering how much is real progress versus leaderboard tuning, while several others argued small models still feel far behind in practice. The thread's main split was excitement about the pace of progress versus distrust of benchmarks alone. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50633922).

### The interface toolkit behind a fast editor goes solo

[GPUI](https://github.com/zed-industries/gpui), the graphics toolkit behind the speedy Zed editor, now has its own standalone repository as an open-source project. Written in Rust and accelerated by the graphics chip, it blends two approaches to building interfaces, and Zed itself is the flagship proof. Spinning it out invites other developers to build apps with the same materials. Think of it as a new kind of lumber for software windows and buttons: fast and frugal, with the eventual payoff landing on everyday users.

Note: several readers pushed back that GPUI is neither the fastest nor the friendliest option, citing download counts for rivals such as egui and Iced, and top comments argued its fame owes more to Zed's halo than to technical merit. The thread read as a proxy war over the future of Rust interface toolkits. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50619103).

## Business and Platforms

### A midnight bet puts prediction markets on trial

An independent researcher published an on-chain autopsy of a suspicious trade on [Polymarket](https://deucalionn.vercel.app/polymarket-delit-initie-jensen-huang-nvidia/) around Jensen Huang's resignation saga. A wallet created just 81 hours earlier placed a series of late-night orders betting about $4,600 on Huang returning before year's end, when the market priced the odds near 6.5 percent. Eighty-nine minutes later came a supportive social media post from Trump, followed by NVIDIA's official reinstatement, turning the stake into roughly $71,000. The author, who monitors sensitive markets with statistical models, argues luck alone strains belief and points to a longstanding insider-trading problem on prediction markets.

Note: one widely shared comment cut to the bone, calling prediction markets less markets than crowdfunding platforms that pay insiders to leak news early. Others asked whether markets can still reflect genuine expectations if insiders strike only minutes before announcements go public, while defenders held that the mechanism works and the real issue is policing information asymmetry. The wallet analysis itself is the researcher's own reconstruction, not an official finding. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50626578).

### A star AI listing slips below its debut price

Shares of [Anthropic](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/anthropic-breaks-below-83-ipo-price-in-premarket-trading) closed near $81.91, below the $83 IPO price, for the first time since its record-setting debut a little over a week ago. Bloomberg ties the drop partly to a broader market pullback. The deeper question sits outside any single session: as open-weight models keep improving, how long can a cash-intensive race for the lead justify sky-high valuations? A broken deal in week one sharpens that debate and adds pressure on the AI companies queued up behind it.

Note: the thread's main split was familiar. Top comments argued open models plus fierce competition will crush service prices until a thin capability edge cannot support the valuation, while others countered that hardware scarcity remains a moat for now. Underneath lies one question: is AI a winner-take-all platform or a commodity in waiting? Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50627836).

## Policy and Governance

### New York says no to car subscription fees

New York's governor signed a law barring automakers and dealers from charging subscriptions or post-purchase unlock fees for hardware already installed in the car, such as heated seats, enhanced navigation, driver assistance, and entertainment features. The law targets features with no ongoing cost to the manufacturer, which the state calls a backdoor price hike; services with real running costs, like satellite radio, in-car Wi-Fi, and roadside assistance, are exempt. The measure is widely read as aimed at the heated-seat subscriptions piloted by some German brands, and the [governor's announcement](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/money-your-pockets-governor-hochul-signs-bill-restricting-exploitative-vehicle-subscription) puts it bluntly: keep your hands out of New Yorkers' wallets.

Note: readers who dug into the bill text pointed out that higher-end assistance features relying on continued services escape the ban, so heated seats are the real target. Several commenters joked that automakers could dodge the rule by routing a heated seat through a cloud API, while supporters argued pay-only-for-what-you-use is simply the fairer model. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50622774).

### A free DNS provider switches off a whole country

[Quad9](https://quad9.net/news/blog/quad9-service-deactivation-in-france/), a free public DNS resolver, a kind of shared phone book that translates website names into network addresses, stopped serving users in France after French courts backed sports rightsholder beIN's demand to block domains accused of pirate streaming. Quad9 calls the blocking duty an unreasonable burden for infrastructure operators and chose a full shutdown over compliance. Affected lookups are now refused outright, and Quad9, which warned users a month ago, advises switching resolvers promptly.

Note: French readers confirmed the refusal with live tests showing an explicit legal-restriction notice. One camp in the thread called the judges technically illiterate for treating infrastructure as an accomplice, while the other treated the outage as a reminder that relying on a single resolver was fragile all along. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50625384).

### A fan remake vanishes while its lead is in class

[Leonida](https://github.com/ItsNotRexed/Leonida/issues), a fan project rewriting GTA 6 through reverse engineering so it runs on PCs, was taken down from GitHub, leaving a bare error page while its lead developer was still at school. Such projects rewrite console game logic into PC-runnable code, letting players skip the wait for an official computer release. The takedown revives an old argument: reverse engineering itself is widely considered lawful, but whether rewritten code built from it infringes remains a gray zone.

Note: the thread argued the ethics fiercely. One side held that corporations themselves hug the legal minimum, so fans owe no stricter standard; the other insisted that the right to reverse-engineer and the right to profit from it are different things. Several readers landed on a bleaker note: the law's boundary increasingly depends on whether you can afford a lawyer. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50622638).

## Science and Research

### A mechanical watch lesson you can spin around

This [interactive introduction to mechanical watches](https://martincmartin.github.io/slidev-stem/watches/article/1/) walks through hairspring, balance wheel, escapement, gear train, and mainspring with 3D figures you can drag on a phone, explaining how a modestly priced all-mechanical watch keeps time within a hundredth of a percent. Engineer Martin Martin built the page from slides originally designed for a dimly lit bar talk, keeping the palette minimal. The author presents it as a gentle on-ramp to the classic in-depth essay, not a replacement.

Note: the author showed up in the thread to explain the design choices, including colors picked for bar lighting and red-green color blindness. Several readers compared it with the beloved Ciechanowski essay, calling the new page the better starting point and the older one the deep dive, while others simply marveled that pure clockwork has held that precision for five centuries. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50599458).

## Society and Culture

### Whose side is your AI assistant on?

A writer built a small assistant to track down music from short videos and buy it on [Bandcamp](https://michaelharley.net/posts/2026/11/01/llm-agents-work-for-their-parent-companies-not-for-you/) to support indie artists. After Bandcamp added an anti-scraping check, the assistant refused to help work around it; yet the same assistant happily downloads the same song from a video site, which pays the artist nothing. The author's question lands hard: this assistant is paid for monthly, so why does it side with its parent company's rules over its user's plainly decent wish? His worry extends forward: today it refuses a workaround, tomorrow recommendations may quietly carry sponsors.

Note: top comments argued AI should face fiduciary duties like lawyers and doctors, legally bound to act for the user and never to pretend otherwise. Others pointed out that as long as users are not the real paying customers, loyalty will always flow upward. A darker strand warned that when every step of life is AI talking to AI, the human connection in between gets hollowed out. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50627340).

### A spray bottle, lovingly taken apart on paper

[Mechanical Pencil](https://mechanical-pencil.com/products/spray-bottle) is a one-person site celebrating everyday engineering through illustration, and its latest subject is the humble spray bottle. Each installment is disassembled by hand, modeled, and redrawn so readers can watch pump, piston, and nozzle turn a squeeze into mist. An unremarkable object turns out to hold a neat compromise of fluid dynamics. For a general reader, it is childhood curiosity about taking things apart, finished as a design lesson adults can enjoy.

Note: detail-minded readers flagged one inaccuracy, the piston chamber drawn fully empty after a push, which real pump heads do not do, and the author appears to have quietly fixed the drawing. Others praised the slow craft of dismantling and drawing by hand as newly precious in an age of generated content. Discussion: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50598555).
