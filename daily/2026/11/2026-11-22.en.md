## Tech and Products

### [Worker 755: A Game Where You Are the AI's Intern](https://github.com/gulivan/worker755)

[Worker 755](https://github.com/gulivan/worker755) flips the org chart: a real large model plays your manager and treats you as its subagent, throwing terminal tasks at you on a 90-second clock, then reviewing, rejecting, and grading your work. There are two workplaces, a clean sandbox and a legacy production box with a live database, where one wrong command can wipe out the employer's family photos. The author open-sourced everything, moved the terminal into a browser worker, and added quotas and isolation so one player cannot take down the server.

Top comments loved the joke, including the thread asking whether 755 comes from chmod 755, which the author says was a lucky random pick. Several readers found the best part is watching the AI manager confidently approve broken work, and the author added local-model support overnight in response to feedback, which HN took as very much in the spirit of the site.

### [Assume Your Prompts Are Stored: OpenRouter Flags Upstream Providers](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/)

[OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/), the router sitting between apps and model providers, said security researchers using canary tokens (planted markers that trigger an alert when copied or stored) found some inference providers were not honoring zero-data-retention promises. The company says it offboarded three providers, stripped 125 endpoints of zero-retention status, notified affected customers, and will run this kind of probing continuously. Named vendors with public statements include Atlas Cloud, Novita, Phala, and Together, while OpenRouter admits not every affected provider has posted publicly.

The thread's main split was between resignation and pragmatism. Many readers argued anything sent over the wire should be assumed recorded, so sensitive work belongs on local open-weight models. Others sympathized with OpenRouter for not naming everyone outright, citing defamation risk, and pushed for technical guarantees through confidential computing instead of a line in a contract.

### [Upload2Shell: One Crafted File to Own Your Printer Queue](https://pwn.ai/upload2shell)

The pwn.ai team disclosed [CVE-2026-125396](https://pwn.ai/upload2shell), nicknamed Upload2Shell, a use-after-free bug in Ghostscript's font loader that escapes its sandbox and runs commands. Ghostscript hides behind print spoolers, email thumbnails, and image converters, so a file disguised as a plain image can reach it through ImageMagick. The timeline runs from discovery in July, a WordPress fix in August, an upstream release in September, distro patches from Debian and Ubuntu, a Google production fix, and a public CVE ID in November. Demonstrated routes included Debian print queues, Roundcube previews, WordPress plugin forms, and wiki uploads, where merely viewing a message could be enough.

Longtime readers noted PostScript is itself a full programming language, so sandboxing it has always been thin. The practical takeaway in the comments was to isolate anything that touches untrusted files, from lightweight syscall filters up to full virtual machines. A few joked that AI-assisted bug hunting means branded vulnerability names are the new normal.

## Business and Platforms

### [Japan's Big Three Share Parts to Fight China's Price War](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a74490092/toyota-nissan-honda-standardize-parts-lower-costs/)

Japan's auto lobby announced that [Toyota, Nissan, and Honda](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a74490092/toyota-nissan-honda-standardize-parts-lower-costs/) will unify quality rules for steel, resins, and wiring-harness colors starting next year, expanding later to more of the car. Chairman Koji Sato spoke of a shared sense of crisis, with savings to be funneled into innovation. Talks begin with domestic suppliers before going global. The report frames it as catch-up: rivals with deep vertical integration, above all in China, set costs Japan cannot match with business as usual.

Commenters argued less about fasteners and more about strategy. The popular view held that standardization saves money but cannot fix a late start in batteries and pure-electric platforms. Several readers with repair experience asked the grounded question: will shared parts actually make fixes cheaper and easier to find.

### [What Happened to Silicon Valley? Tim O'Reilly's Forty-Year Reckoning](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/what-happened-to-silicon-valley/)

In a lecture turned long essay, [What Happened to Silicon Valley?](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/what-happened-to-silicon-valley/), O'Reilly founder Tim O'Reilly contrasts his own slow build, funded by paying customers, with a Valley rewired by shareholder capitalism. He divides rents into rising-tide rents that lift everyone during innovation and robber-baron rents extracted through market power later, pointing to ad-choked search and shopping pages. He also contrasts the operating economy, where a dollar of profit is a dollar, with the betting economy, where a dollar of profit becomes many dollars of market value, and warns that blitzscaling turns capital, not customers, into the kingmaker.

HN largely bought the diagnosis as incentives rather than villains. The disagreement was over remedies: revive antitrust and consumer law, or admit markets are designed artifacts and rewrite the rules. Old-timers added a caution against nostalgia, noting the early web had its hustlers too, but value creation still won.

### [Backblaze Rewrites Its Privacy Policy, and Backup Fans Get Nervous](https://www.neowin.net/news/backblaze-customers-worried-of-personal-data-salesharing-after-policy-update/)

Neowin reports that [Backblaze](https://www.neowin.net/news/backblaze-customers-worried-of-personal-data-salesharing-after-policy-update/) updated its privacy policy to say tracking tech may amount to selling or sharing personal data for cross-context advertising, covering identifiers, network activity, and geolocation, effective December 1. Users who entrust the company with whole-disk backups noticed fast. The same report clarifies the policy covers account and website behavior data, while backed-up file contents sit outside California's sale-or-share definition and, the company says, are not sold. Opt-outs live under footer privacy choices and browser privacy signals.

HN split into pragmatists and the furious. Pragmatists read it as boilerplate forced by California litigation over analytics pixels, aimed at website tracking rather than backup contents. The other camp recommended client-side encryption with tools like Restic or Cryptomator before upload, so the policy hardly matters. A recalled anecdote about analytics scripts once leaking file paths kept the skeptics skeptical.

## Policy and Governance

### [The Smokestack Behind the Chatbot: Anthropic's Fossil-Heavy Power Mix](https://betterdatacenterproject.com/2026/11/16/how-ai-is-powered-a-breakdown-of-byop-and-grid-power/)

The nonprofit [Better Data Center Project](https://betterdatacenterproject.com/2026/11/16/how-ai-is-powered-a-breakdown-of-byop-and-grid-power/) used Anthropic as a case study, estimating about 9.6 gigawatts of contracted compute, with roughly 87 percent of the power from gas and 11 percent from coal in 2028 and almost no direct US renewable commitments. Its mid-case puts annual carbon dioxide near 32 million tonnes, on par with California's power-sector emissions, plus thousands of tonnes of nitrogen oxides and fine particles worth billions in health damages. The authors separate behind-the-meter gas from marginal grid plants and note some compute sits on facilities still running temporary turbines. Anthropic has said it follows air permits and requires emission controls, but the report sees fossil fuels dominating in practice.

Commenters fought over framing as much as numbers. Some called atomic-bomb analogies for waste heat cheap rhetoric, while others noted nearly all data-center power ends up as heat anyway. The most upvoted line of thought held that demand is not the bottleneck, slow clean-power permitting is, with district heating, faster nuclear, wind, and solar offered as better outlets than scolding consumption alone.

### [Email, but for Social Media: Germany's Open Social Web Bet](https://osw-alliance.net/en/)

The [Open Social Web Alliance](https://osw-alliance.net/en/), backed by Germany's Heinrich Boll Foundation, sketches a roadmap for social apps that interoperate like email over open protocols (ActivityPub and AT Protocol, the shared languages letting different apps exchange posts), so users pick their own algorithms and take posts and graphs with them. It asks the German government to host a summit, adopt a charter, fund core infrastructure by spring, publish a social-web health report, and move public broadcasters in first. The paper counts about 13 million active fediverse users and 43 million registered ATmosphere accounts, blaming slow growth on infrastructure, governance, funding, appeal, and public mandate.

HN mostly jeered at manifesto-first politics, calling the site fluffy and short on anything shippable. Defenders of the direction argued the ad model structurally rewards anger, so protocol-level portability is still worth wanting. The shared punchline was to ship a client people love before writing another declaration.

### [Europe Loosens Chemical Rules as the Science Gets Darker](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/opinion/article/2026/11/22/as-science-increasingly-shows-the-role-chemical-substances-play-in-lifestyle-diseases-europe-weakens-its-regulations_6786595_23.html)

A Le Monde column cites a new WHO-UNEP assessment: just seven groups of endocrine disruptors cost Europe on the order of 1 trillion euros a year. Prenatal pesticide exposure tracks with intellectual disability, plasticizers with endometriosis and male infertility, and forever chemicals with obesity and thyroid trouble. The author argues that blaming lifestyle choices [lets regulators off the hook while Europe weakens its chemical rules](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/opinion/article/2026/11/22/as-science-increasingly-shows-the-role-chemical-substances-play-in-lifestyle-diseases-europe-weakens-its-regulations_6786595_23.html). The piece sits behind a paywall, but the headline figures are public and point at everyday exposure through food, air, water, and home goods.

The thread's main split was over trade-offs. Some said Europe bans chemicals at home while importing cheaper food grown under looser rules, effectively outsourcing pollution. Others pointed to border instruments like carbon tariffs as a template for holding imports to similar standards. Few disputed that exposures in pregnancy and childhood deserve priority, even while questioning model quality behind the trillion-euro math.

## Science and Research

### [OpenAI Opens Its Perplexity Curve and Asks for Shared Speed Limits](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/)

In [Perplexity and Intelligence](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/), OpenAI publishes internal pretraining perplexity, a measure of how well the model predicts the next token, lower is better, from GPT-3.5 onward. New capabilities in math and cybersecurity arrived once losses fell below 0.8, which the author reads as emergence from better compression rather than clever prompting. The essay warns the next window could bring unknown abilities while current chain-of-thought monitoring degrades, and proposes joint transparency plus common development limits until oversight catches up. It also lists weaknesses: hard cross-lab comparison, no direct task mapping, no credit for reasoning gains, and an unknown floor as data entropy bites.

HN liked having one simple long-run yardstick but questioned the dataset. Several readers noted the curve looks code-heavy, so part of the drop may come from feeding more code rather than general intelligence rising. Top comments separated base-model perplexity from post-training behavior, where reinforcement learning often makes measured perplexity worse, and asked for multidimensional evaluation.

### [Your Brain Is Not a Computer: A Neuroscientist Against Conscious AI](https://www.noemamag.com/the-mythology-of-conscious-ai/)

Sussex consciousness researcher Anil Seth argues in [The Mythology of Conscious AI](https://www.noemamag.com/the-mythology-of-conscious-ai/) that intelligence is about doing while consciousness is about being, and anthropocentrism plus anthropomorphism make fluent language look like feeling. He offers four strikes against computational functionalism: brains lack a clean software-hardware split, continuous and stochastic dynamics fall outside Turing's frame, life's self-maintenance may matter, and simulation is not instantiation. Even if silicon minds never wake up, he warns, seemingly conscious systems already distort ethics, tempting us to grant rights pointlessly or to brutalize our own minds by switching feelings off.

The comments replayed the classic stalemate. One side said consciousness cannot be tested, so ruling machine experience out is premature. The other put the burden of proof on claimants and warned against smuggling dualism in through biology. Engineers in the thread mostly agreed substrate likely matters but silicon is still physics, so exclusion in advance is unwarranted. The practical consensus was to handle anthropomorphic manipulation first.

### [Stulle Time: A Tiny German Sandwich Simulator](https://stulle.app/)

[Stulle.app](https://stulle.app/) recreates the German open-faced sandwich, the Stulle: tap the butter pot, spread with finger or mouse, drag toppings from the rail, twist to rotate, photograph the result, and share a link. Hints come in German and English, with realistic shadows and wobble. A Stulle is one slice with toppings, the author notes, while two slices make a Klappstulle, a form familiar across the Nordics and Basque country too.

HN responded with towering stacks that lagged browsers and earnest UX debate. Readers shared a similar World's Best Sandwich project, fixed performance live, and requested two-slice builds and diagonal cuts. Several agreed the rough low-poly charm, closer to the old playful web than photorealism, is exactly why the interaction delights.

### [Fifteen Years in Web Dev, Dreaming of Firmware](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50555002)

One developer [asked HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50555002) how feasible a late move from a decade of full-stack web work back to C, C++, and Rust-flavored embedded roots really is. Replies turned the short post into a field guide: tiny memories, hard deadlines, logic analyzers instead of breakpoints, a different mindset. Job advice centered on leading with early native experience and closing the gap with hobby builds and referrals. Skeptics warned that systems with no logs and no debugger grind people down and suggested trying a dev board first.

The dominant advice was to just start building. One camp said to run from tool-poor environments, while embedded veterans countered that scopes and analyzers are the real eyes in that world. The through-line was that steady public work and relationships now matter more than grinding puzzles.
